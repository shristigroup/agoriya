How to Pick a Field Force Automation App

Last checked November 2026 · What a field force automation app should do, which features are optional, and 12 apps to look at

Who needs field force automation software

Any business with people who work away from a desk can use it. It matters most to manpower and staffing companies, typically blue collar workforce providers, that supply field staff to pharma, FMCG, BFSI and logistics companies. These companies cannot see their people at work, yet their clients expect proof that the staff turned up and did the job.

Most of them manage this on WhatsApp today. Each team has a group. Staff post a photo and an update from the field and tag their supervisor. It is quick to start and everyone already has the app, but the data is scattered across chats. Nobody can say how many people were present last Tuesday without scrolling, and nothing is ready for payroll.

What changes with a field force automation app

  • One place for all data. The owner has attendance, locations, distance travelled and customer visits in one place, instead of in many chat groups.
  • Easy payroll. Export the data to Excel when you need it, and run payroll from clean attendance records.
  • Real-time supervision. Supervisors see updates as they happen and get notifications, instead of waiting for someone to post in a group.

Essential features

These are the features to expect from any app you shortlist. If an app lacks one, ask why.

1Attendance

Staff punch in and out from the phone, with the time and place recorded.

2Face / selfie attendance

Checks who is punching in with a selfie or face recognition, to stop buddy punching.

3Live location

Records where each person is while on duty, using the phone's GPS, so supervisors can see where staff are right now.

4Distance

Works out the kilometres each person travelled in a day, for travel claims.

5Route timeline

Replays the day's path on a map in order, so you see where staff went and when. Only a few apps do this.

6Task, order & visit management

Assign tasks, take customer orders or record customer visits from the phone. Full task and order management is the most complete option. Visit logging, with a form, notes or photos, is a cheaper alternative that covers most teams.

7Expense logging

Staff log travel and other expenses with bills from the phone, and managers approve them, instead of collecting paper bills.

8Roster, shift & leave management

Plan who works which shift and track leave. A roster also lets the app send a notification to both the employee and the supervisor when attendance is not marked on time, so gaps are caught the same day.

9Export to Excel

All attendance, distance and visit data can be downloaded as a spreadsheet. This makes payroll do-it-yourself: you run salary from clean data in your own tool or sheet, without paying for a built-in payroll module.

10Real-time notifications

Supervisors are told when someone punches in, punches out or logs a visit, so they act on the day instead of reading a report later.

Good-to-have features

Many apps add these, and they are often what makes a plan cost more. Take them only if your business needs them today, because you pay for them whether or not your team uses them.

  • Geofencing: Limits punch-in to a set place, or marks attendance automatically when staff enter or leave it. A selfie check-in together with the recorded location can pretty much compensate for it, so it is rarely worth paying more for.
  • Customizable forms: Build your own forms for audits, checklists or reports. Useful when a standard visit form does not fit what your client asks for.
  • Payroll processing: The app calculates salary from attendance, leave and deductions. Because export to Excel is essential and already makes payroll doable on your own, built-in payroll is good to have, not a must.
  • Biometric, QR or kiosk attendance: Needs devices installed at each site, which is expensive, and works only at fixed places. Mobile attendance is much easier and more efficient for field staff.

Check the price carefully

Prices are quoted per user per month, but the real cost depends on the fine print: the plan that includes route and distance, a minimum number of users, a base fee, a setup fee or an add-on for each feature. Work out the yearly cost for your own headcount before you decide.

  • No setup fee: No one-time onboarding or per-user setup charge.
  • Free tier: A free plan you can use without paying, usually with a limit on the number of users.

How to choose

  1. List the essential features above and the good-to-haves you really need.
  2. Shortlist two or three apps that list those features publicly.
  3. Work out the yearly cost for your number of users, with base and setup fees included.
  4. Look at the app store ratings and how many people rated it. A high score from a handful of ratings means little.
  5. Try it with one team on the phones your staff actually use before you roll it out to everyone.

How the score works (out of 100)

  • Features: 5 points if listed, 3 if partly met (amber), 1 if not. The 8 essential features count double; the 4 good-to-have features and the two price terms (no setup fee, free tier) count once.
  • Price: per user per month for a 30-user team. Green below $2, amber $2 to $3, red above $3. Counts once.
  • Ratings: each store rating counts once. Green 4.5 and above, amber 4.0 and above, red below, grey (1 point) if there is no listing.
  • Ticks: ✓ means listed publicly. A dash means we could not find it, not that it is certainly missing.
  • Merged features: visit logging alone counts for "Task, order & visit management". A roster or leave management counts for "Roster, shift & leave management".
  • Payroll: amber "Excel export" means no payroll module but data export to Excel (TrackFolks and OnthegO.ai; not checked for every vendor).
  • Not in the table: real-time notifications are essential, but most vendors do not state them.
  • Prices: annual plan, dollars at ₹95 = $1, excluding 18% GST, coloured by the top of each vendor's range. Ratings show stars and the number of ratings.
