How to Choose a Field Force Tracking App: Full Comparison Guide

Last checked 25 October 2026 · Fourteen apps, compared feature by feature

Apps for tracking field teams range from general time clocks that added GPS, to Indian attendance and payroll apps, to purpose-built field sales tools. No app wins on every dimension: the cheapest may lack selfie checks, the one with the deepest GPS may have no leave or payroll. This guide lists the factors that matter, then compares fourteen apps feature by feature, with cost per user in USD.

The factors that matter

These follow the columns of the comparison table below, in the same order.

1Selfie or photo at punch-in

A selfie or face check shows who punched in, which reduces buddy punching. Check whether it is included in the plan you will pay for or only in a higher tier.

2Location at punch-in

Capturing GPS when someone punches in and out shows where the punch happened. It is the minimum most field teams need, and some apps charge extra for it or restrict it to higher plans.

3Live location

Live location answers "where is this person right now?" It is useful for dispatch and supervision. It is often an add-on or a top-tier feature, and usually works only while the person is clocked in.

4Route history and distance

A recorded route answers "where did they go today, and how far?" It matters for mileage claims, proof of visits and beat compliance. A route needs continuous tracking, which uses more battery than a location at punch-in. Some apps only join the punch points with straight lines.

5Geofencing

Geofencing restricts punches to a site, or punches people in and out automatically when they enter or leave one. It is usually a mid-tier feature.

6Visit logging

Field sales and service teams need to log each customer visit with a location, notes and photos. General time clocks often lack this.

7Expenses

Expense claims, receipts and mileage reimbursement save the back-and-forth of paper bills. Check whether they are built in or only a mileage report.

8Leave and payroll

HR-led businesses want leave balances and payroll in the same tool. Field-first apps often leave these out, so decide which side matters more.

9Works offline

With poor coverage, an app that cannot queue punches and locations loses data. Check how each app behaves without a signal.

10Free plan

"Free" often means a trial, or a free plan without GPS. Look at the user limit and at which features are switched off.

11Cost per user per month, and what it covers

Base prices can mislead: some vendors charge extra for location, charge a base fee, or put GPS in a higher tier. Compare the plan that includes the features you need, and note minimum seats and flat fees. The last two columns show the approximate cost for a 30-user team and what that price covers.

12iPhone and Android apps, and their ratings

Your team will not all own the same phone, so check that both apps exist. Store ratings are the most credible public signal of how well an app actually works: look at the star rating and at the number of ratings, since a high score from a handful of reviews means little.
