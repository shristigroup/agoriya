Pick the category by the work your people do, then pick the app by team size, phone reliability and price. Most buyers lose time because they compare apps from different categories, such as a payroll app against a dispatch tool, as if they did the same job.

This guide covers four categories and 37 apps, Indian and global, read through Google Play, the Apple App Store, G2, Capterra and Trustpilot on 17 and 23 November 2026. It is written by TrackFolks, which makes one of the apps listed (a field force app), so read the TrackFolks entry with that in mind. The research was done with AI assistance and the review counts are samples, not full audits.

The line between them is the customer. FFA and SFA manage your own employees' movement and sales activity. FSM manages jobs for your customers, so it adds dispatch, quotes, invoices and payments that FFA does not have. HR and workforce tools start from attendance and payroll, then add location at punch time; they rarely follow a person along a route.

Field Force Automation (FFA)

Choose FFA if you pay people to be on the road and need proof of where they were, what they did and how far they travelled. Every FFA app in this guide gets the basics right, so the real differences are distance accuracy, battery use, iPhone quality and price. Battery drain and wrong kilometre counts are the top complaint for almost every app below, and none is free of them.

Who it fits: companies with reps, collection agents, inspectors or merchandisers, where travel allowance is paid on distance and managers want daily visit proof.

Essential features, in order of importance:

  • Attendance with a selfie or location stamp, so a punch-in cannot be faked from home.
  • Live location and a route timeline, so a manager can see where someone was during the day.
  • Accurate distance, because it drives reimbursement. Test this on a real route during the trial.
  • Visit, task and order management with proof of visit.
  • Expense logging and Excel export. Built-in payroll is nice to have, not a must.
  • Low battery use and offline punches, because field phones are cheap and networks drop.

Sales Force Automation (SFA)

Choose SFA if your reps sell physical goods through retail outlets and distributors, and you need orders, beats and stock visibility, not just attendance. SFA apps are heavier than FFA apps: they model outlets, schemes and distributors, so they take weeks to roll out and are usually sold by quote.

Who it fits: FMCG, CPG and consumer durables companies with route-based sales teams and distributor networks. If you only need to know where reps were, an FFA app is simpler and far cheaper.

Essential features, in order of importance:

  • Beat and route planning with outlet visit proof (GPS stamp and photo).
  • Order capture in the field, including offline orders that sync later.
  • Outlet master data, retail audits and scheme or promotion tracking.
  • Distributor and secondary sales visibility, so orders, stock and claims reconcile.
  • Dashboards by region, rep and outlet, with attendance and live location as a supporting feature.

Field Service Management (FSM)

Choose FSM if your people go to your customers' homes or sites to do jobs, and you need to schedule them, quote the work, finish it and get paid. FSM is the only category here that handles the customer side: dispatch, estimates, invoices and payments. If you only track employees, FSM is more software than you need.

Who it fits: plumbers, HVAC, electrical, installation, repair and maintenance businesses, from one-truck operators to enterprises with hundreds of technicians.

Essential features, in order of importance:

  • Scheduling and dispatch on a calendar or map, with technician status updates.
  • Quotes or estimates, then invoices and payments from the technician's phone.
  • Customer communication: appointment reminders and "on the way" messages.
  • A technician app that works on site: job details, photos, forms and checklists, offline use.
  • Accounting and payment integrations, such as QuickBooks.
  • GPS location of technicians and routing, which matters but is a supporting feature here.

The global FSM apps below were built around US-style markets and dollar pricing. If you are in India, check payment, tax and integration support before you commit. Ratings are Google Play, the US App Store and G2, because that is where these apps' customers are. FieldEZ is an Indian vendor with very little app-store data, so it is judged mainly on G2 and Capterra.

HR and workforce tools with remote attendance and location

Choose this category if your main job is running attendance, leave and payroll for a team that is partly remote or deskless, and location is a check, not the product. These tools stamp a place when someone punches in or out. Most do not follow a person along a route, so they cannot replace an FFA app for travel allowance or visit proof.

Who it fits: employers with remote, field or site staff, who want attendance, leave and payroll or timesheets in one place.

Essential features, in order of importance:

  • Mobile attendance with a location stamp or geofence, and a selfie or face check against buddy punching.
  • Leave, shift and roster management.
  • Timesheets and payroll or payroll-ready exports.
  • Reports for compliance and audit.
  • Live location, which is optional here and often a paid add-on.

Location in this category is usually tied to a plan or sold as an add-on, and it records where someone punched in, not the route they took. Keka, Pocket HRMS and Zoho People put geofencing or location tracking in higher plans, factoHR does too according to third-party listings, Hubstaff charges extra for it, and HROne includes geo-tagging and geofencing in its Basic plan. The entries start with the HR and payroll suites, then the time and attendance tools. For the large HR suites only review counts and plan details were collected, not review text.

Three reading notes:

  • Per-user and flat prices are different things. FSM tools charge by plan, job volume or licence, so a 30-person crew can cost far more than the per-user rows suggest.
  • Check what the price includes. Route tracking, GPS, stop addresses and payroll are add-ons or higher plans on several apps. Hubstaff GPS is the clearest case: $10 becomes about $13.33.
  • TrackFolks example. With the first 10 users free, a 30-user team pays for 20 users: 20 × $1.05 × 12 is about $252 a year, under the $499 cap. This is my arithmetic from the listed price.
  • Indian HR suites price blocks of 50. Pocket HRMS, HROne and factoHR quote a monthly price for the first 50 employees, so the per-user figures above assume at least 50 users.

The bottom line: category first, then app

Pick the category from the job your people do. Only then compare apps, because the wrong category costs you more than the wrong app.

You pay field staff for distance and need visit proof: Field Force Automation. Start with Unolo for the most proven, TrackFolks or dayTrack for the lowest price, and test distance on a real route.

You sell FMCG through outlets and distributors: Sales Force Automation. FieldAssist or Bizom for a large rollout, BeatRoute, Delta or SalesOn for a lighter start, and get written quotes.

You send technicians to customers' sites and invoice the job: Field Service Management. Zoho FSM for the lowest price in India, Jobber or Housecall Pro for small crews, ServiceTitan or Salesforce for large operations.

You mainly need attendance, leave and payroll for remote or deskless staff: an HR or workforce tool. Zoho People, Pocket HRMS or HROne for the lowest cost, Keka or factoHR for a large HR suite, Jibble for a polished time clock, Connecteam for shift teams, Truein for face attendance.

Before you pay any of them:

  • Test on your own phones. Battery use and distance accuracy vary by handset and cannot be judged from ratings.
  • Compare the iPhone and Android ratings separately. FieldSense (2.5 on iPhone), Unolo (3.3) and FieldAssist (3.1) rate far lower on iPhone than on Android.
  • Ask what the price includes. GPS, routes, stop addresses and payroll are often extra, and in HR suites location tracking depends on the plan (Keka's Strength and Growth plans, Pocket HRMS and Zoho People Professional).
  • Read recent reviews. Ratings move: Timeero's Play rating is 3.0 today, well below the 4.7 some older listings show.

Method, sources and limits

This guide was researched with AI assistance on 17 and 23 November 2026 and published by TrackFolks, which sells one of the apps covered. Treat the TrackFolks entry as a vendor's description, not an independent review.

What was read

  • Google Play: up to about 340 reviews per app from the "Most relevant" list, counted by theme. This list skews negative, so counts show what is common, not what share of users complain.
  • Apple App Store: rating, rating count, release date and the 3 to 4 reviews each page shows. Indian apps were read in the India store; global apps in the US store.
  • G2, Capterra and Trustpilot: G2 and Trustpilot were read in the browser. Capterra text comes from pages supplied as screenshots; for Jibble, Timeero and Connecteam only the "Pros and Cons in Reviews" summary block was used.
  • Release dates: the Play "Updated on" date was used as a sign of active development. It can reflect small patches, so it is not proof of new features.
  • 23 November additions: Google Play listings and review samples, India App Store search results for rating counts (the search page shows the count without opening each app), G2 pages for ratings and counts, and the vendor sites of SalesOn and Bizom. Pricing for the HR suites and some SFA apps comes from vendor and third-party listings seen in search results.

Limits

  • Review counts are samples, not audits, and no app was tested hands-on.
  • Reviews were not read for Zoho People, Keka, HROne, factoHR or Pocket HRMS, only counts and plan details; BeatRoute's iPhone page was not read; Bizom's G2 review text would not load. BeatRoute has no Play listing under its own name.
  • Several apps have few reviews on some sources (TrackWick 2 on G2, Lystloc 6 on Capterra, BreezeFSM 15 on Capterra), so their ratings are weak evidence.
  • Prices marked "Vendor page" were read from the vendor's own pricing page on 17 or 23 November, on annual billing, with Indian vendors in rupees at ₹95 to the dollar. Unolo, BreezeFSM and PagarBook publish no price, so their figures come from the TrackFolks comparison page and are unconfirmed. TrackFolks' own price is as TrackFolks states it. OnthegO.ai, FieldSense and Jibble publish different prices by region; the table gives the India or discounted regional rate and notes the other.
  • Plans change often. Confirm any price with the vendor before you buy.
  • Apps added on 23 November had to have about 100 or more Google Play reviews and a Play update within 12 months. That left out SFA 360 (33 reviews), Bizintel SFA (38), GoSales (50+ downloads), SimpliDOTS SFA (no review count shown), Toolyt (little recent review evidence on G2 and the app stores) and SANeFORCE's SAN SFE (not updated on Play since 1 Sep 2025). Leaving an app out does not mean it is poor.
  • FieldEZ has very little app-store data (15 Play reviews, 9 iPhone ratings in the India store), so it is judged on G2 and Capterra. Test it before adopting.
  • Hubstaff's Android app (last updated 31 Dec 2025) and SalesOn's (3 Jan 2026) will pass 12 months without an update in early January 2027.
  • SalesOn's price differs between its Play listing and its own site, and the Pocket HRMS, HROne, factoHR, Salesdiary, FieldEZ and SAN SALES prices come from listings seen in search results, not all of which were opened.
